# Curriculum gaps and educational review

This document separates current code from proposals. Severity reflects impact on breadth and learning clarity, not code quality or whether an activity is a useful starter.

## Strongest current areas

- **Context breadth:** Tell Me has 81 approved bilingual picture scenes across nine settings, with open-ended prompts, optional grown-up support and no speech scoring.
- **Spatial concepts:** Prepositions has 52 reviewed examples across 13 relations and multiple canonical characters/props. It is the largest focused relation set.
- **Time sequencing:** Months, Seasons and Wilma cover names, local/cyclic order, missing items, before/after, building sequences and selected cross-links. Their concepts have canonical identifiers and separate persistence.
- **Listening progression:** Follow the Instructions has six illustrated targets and independent one-step and ordered two-step modes with replay, hints and saved sessions.
- **Growing native foundations:** The sorting reducer is used by Colours, Animals and Seasons Match; sequence assembly is used by Wash Hands; native progress/session infrastructure supports many of the newer experiences.

## Discoverability, explanation, interaction and feedback

- **Discoverability is strongest** where pictures carry the task and Replay/Help is explicit (Tell Me, Seasons/Months Explore, Follow’s illustrated targets). Reading independence is not required in core native activities, though text-only Wash Hands cards need an adult/replay for a pre-reader when speech is unavailable.
- **Concept introduction is uneven.** Wilma and Months Explore let a child browse and replay names; Seasons Explore has four canonical descriptions; Colour Sort and Animal Groups begin directly with sorting and short instructions. A child can succeed in Colour Sort by matching visual category colors without first learning the names “red/blue.”
- **Interaction quality is mixed by design.** Native sorting, sequence assembly, Months/Seasons ordering and Clock Make This Time require action. The five native Maths rounds and many legacy activities are still answer selection. Tell Me is conversational rather than quiz-like.
- **Feedback is generally retry-oriented**, with hints/replay and no penalty/lives. Some feedback is generic (“Try again”) and some is instructional (“Look at its colour”, “Think about the number just before…”). Shared progress is not universal: Tell Me intentionally has no score/progress, and legacy exercises retain their own checkpoint behavior.
- **Context quality varies.** Prepositions and Tell Me have detailed situated scenes; native quantity activities show abstract dots; Colours uses simple shapes; Animal Groups gives habitat labels but no ecological context; legacy Math/Actions uses emoji.
